POD: three lengths from one set of parts

Drawn to scale from one set of numbers. A galvanised steel frame, built as the market builds capsules, clad in 20 mm TRC panels made with RTARIUM (the one innovation: thin concrete that carries the load the aluminium skin never did), rockwool behind them. The two end modules are the same, mirrored, and so are the two long sides; the pod grows by panels added in the middle. The roof band carries the electrics, the kicker at the base the plumbing. Glass in blue, frosted glass dashed.

The rule: fixed ends, panels added in the middle

End module 3000 mm (the cut corners and the panoramic window), door bay 1000, middle panels 1000 each. In red, the middle panels: each length adds two. A wall panel 1000 wide and about 2.1 m high is cut from one 1220 × 2400 RTARIUM sheet; a roof panel across the pod from one 3400 sheet.

The wall, roof and base

Schematic cut across the pod; layers thickened to be read. The panel is RTARIUM's stud-frame cladding: sprayed in a mould with the mesh in it, ferrules cast in, its galvanised stud frame bolted on, then hung on the pod's frame. Thicknesses beyond the panel's 20 mm are ours: CONFIRM.

The panel, magnified

RTARIUM: two faces of basalt mesh woven together and held 10 mm apart by spacer fibres; the concrete fills the space between them and locks round it. As in RTARIUM's own 20 mm panel: 5 mm of cover beyond each face. The mesh comes in sheets 1220 mm wide and 2.4, 3.4 or 5.8 m long.
