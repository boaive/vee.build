thickness. and same cladding like fitting works here too."

Second message, with two sketches of the pod's long side: "the galvanized
steel frame -> skeleton -> rockwool as insulation attached to trc -> stud
frame cladding style, made with concreate with the use of rtarium mesh. and
by here we have covered insulation and exterior, for interior we may use
some kind of interior precast or what they use in general. ... the pod is
equal in opposite side, in other words mirror able. ... we don't cover the
bottom as we cover the top, we have kicker in the bottom where plumbing
goes. electrical goes to the top." On the sketch, between the door and the
far end: "increase in length, introduce new panels in here".

Third message: "those things are followed by how standard market works.
the only innovation here is trc. concreate panels are the one that makes
this strong. and it is made by rtarium", with RTARIUM's technical data
(`D:\float you thrive\rtarium v2\assets\data and ai center\RTARIUM
TECHNICAL DATA - WEBSITE.pdf`) and the RTARIUM site project beside this one.

Read together: frame, insulation, lining, glazing and fit-out are the
market's usual capsule, as in the video. The one innovation is the skin:
TRC panels, thin concrete made strong by RTARIUM's mesh, in place of the
video's aluminium cladding ("no metal or wood" is the skin).

## 2. How the POD is built

**The innovation: the TRC panel.** RTARIUM by HEMISPHERE ONE (the name as
their own site sets it; HEMISPHERE ONE is also this site's "Powered by") is
a three-dimensional woven spacer mesh of basalt fibre: two faces of mesh
woven together and held apart by spacer fibres, so the concrete fills the
space between them and locks round the reinforcement in three directions.
It is non-corrosive and alkali-resistant, and replaces glass or steel fibre
in thin high-performance concrete. From their data sheet (the client's
figures; the RTARIUM project keeps the margin notes as corrections and
flags the rest):

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Mesh sheets | 1220 mm wide; 2.4, 3.4 or 5.8 m long; 3, 6, 10 or 15 mm thick |
| Mesh | > 50 kN/m tensile; 79,600 MPa modulus; 3.4 % elongation; 820 °C sustained, melts at 1460–1500 °C |
| Concrete with it | axial tensile 19.7 MPa (about 20; plain concrete 2–5); elastic to 18.3 MPa; tensile modulus 64 GPa; against steel fibre in UHPC: tensile 16.2 → 20.9 MPa, flexural 28.6 → 50.6 MPa |

The POD's skin panel, as RTARIUM draws its own 20 mm panel: the 10 mm
mesh with 5 mm of concrete beyond each face (the panel magnified in the
drawings). Made as their **stud-frame cladding**: a mould cut to the
profile, concrete sprayed in with the mesh cut, stitched and laid in it
(the mesh is rigid: corners are cut and stitched, never bent), ferrule
inserts cast in at the back, the panel's own galvanised stud frame bolted
to the ferrules, cured and demoulded; then hung on the pod's frame, with
flex anchors for its movement in heat (their site map). That is the
developer's "skeleton ... stud frame cladding style".

At an assumed 2,400 kg/m³ (RTARIUM's project assumes the same, flagged) a
20 mm skin is about 48 kg/m²: a 1000 × 2100 wall panel about 100 kg before
its studs and rockwool. Heavier than the video's aluminium, far lighter
than precast. CONFIRM the tested density.

**The wall, outside in** (section drawing):

1. The TRC panel, 20 mm, as above.
2. Rockwool between the studs, against the back of the panel (thickness
   ours: CONFIRM).
3. The skeleton: the panel's galvanised stud frame, on its ferrules.
4. The pod's galvanised steel frame, as in the video; the panels bolt to it.
5. Interior lining, as the market does it (the video's boards), or
   precast: RTARIUM makes 30 mm solid panels, and partitions of 30 mm
   panels either side of a 40 mm stud with the services in between
   (their wall-panel pages). CONFIRM which.

**Roof and base are not alike.** The roof is wrapped as the walls are, TRC
over rockwool with its edges cut, and the ceiling void under it carries the
electrics, lights and AC ("electrical goes to the top"). The base is not
wrapped: under the walls of the middle part a kicker, set back from the
face and left open, carries the plumbing round to where it leaves the pod
("we have kicker in the bottom where plumbing goes"). The end modules close
down over the kicker to their feet (the sketch).

**Mirrored.** The two long sides are the same (the back is the front's
mirror image), and so are the two end modules: one set of frames and panels
serves either side and either end, and a pod can be built handed. On the
back, the door's bay takes a frosted panel over the bathroom (CONFIRM: or a
second door, or a plain panel).

**Fixed ends, panels added in the middle** (the sketch's "increase in
length, introduce new panels in here"). A pod is

    end module 3000 + door bay 1000 + m × middle panel 1000 + end module 3000

with m = 1, 3 and 5 for 8, 10 and 12 m: each length adds two panels. An end
module is the cut corners, the end glazing and the panoramic window; a
middle panel is plain or carries a window (10 m: one, 12 m: two, in the far
room). The 3000 and 1000 are ours, chosen so that the three lengths come
out exact and the door opens onto the core. The mesh fits them: a wall
panel 1000 wide and about 2.1 m high comes out of one 1220 × 2400 sheet,
a roof panel across the pod out of one 3400 sheet. A 1220 panel (the
sheet's full width) would waste less but cannot make 2 m steps: 8, 10 and
12 m would become about 8.0, 10.4 and 12.9. CONFIRM the panel width with
RTARIUM.

**Feet** under the two end modules, as the sketch has them; whether the 12 m
needs a pair under its middle is for the frame's design. Lifting points on
the frame's top (four, as every shell on the site).

**Colours** (ours, after the references): the TRC in light concrete grey,
joints dark; charcoal window surrounds and door; tinted glass; the kicker
dark, so the pod reads as standing above its base.

## 4. The POD page

The references' order in our design system, under our rules (short copy,
no figure we were not given, Coming soon, the form last):

1. **Hero.** The pod side-on, large, live 3D on the studio floor, with the
   code (`POD 12`), "Capsule home." and one line; a length switch 8 · 10 ·
   12 m under it, the pod growing or shrinking by its middle panels.
2. **Title and one line.**
3. **Views and specifications**, side by side: our captures of the live
   model, and the chosen length's two layouts with rooms and area, then
   L × W × H.
4. **Floor plan**, the chosen length, layout A or B, dimensioned.
5. **The panel.** The one thing that is new, given the room it deserves:
   the 20 mm skin cut open in 3D (two mesh faces, the spacers, the
   concrete through them), then hung on the frame; two or three of
   RTARIUM's figures, each CONFIRM, and "RTARIUM by HEMISPHERE ONE".
   Then, briefly, the wall's layers, the electrics in the roof and the
   plumbing in the kicker. Ours; the references have no such block.
6. **Specifications in three tabs**: standard, customisation, optional, one
   line an item, each CONFIRM until the client gives it (the references'
   items are not ours).
7. **Delivery and installation**: delivered whole, set by crane on its feet,
   connected to power, water and drain. No day counts or payment terms
   until the client gives them.
8. **Enquiry form, last**, preset Capsule Pod, with the length.

One page with a length switch keeps the routes. A page a length would add
routes: CLAUDE.md asks first.

## 5. The 3D model

Our own, built from the same parts as the real pod, so that the module rule
is the model: two end modules (mirrored), a door bay, m middle panels, the
roof band, the kicker, the feet. A length is just a count of panels, which
also gives the site its animation for free: on the length switch, or in a
banner, the middle panels slide in and the far end moves out. Separate
glass (lit from inside with `litGlass` once it stands), surrounds, door,
balcony guard and simple furniture blocks seen through the large glass
(`interiorGlass`'s fake room is too small for panes this big). Cut open for
section 5: the TRC, the rockwool, the skeleton and the frame as layers,
the roof void, the kicker with its pipes. The plan drawn on the page is
the same data.
