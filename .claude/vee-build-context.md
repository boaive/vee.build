# VEE.BUILD — business context

## What the business is

VEE.BUILD manufactures a standardised modular / precast concrete construction
system. Modules are cast off-site in an open-air factory, transported, and
assembled on site. The same two modules combine horizontally into different
footprints and stack vertically into different building types.

**One system → many configurations → many building types.**

| Module | Size | Nominal area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shell A | 6 m × 3 m × 3 m | 200 sq.ft. (18 m²) |
| Shell B | 3 m × 3 m × 3 m | 100 sq.ft. (9 m²) |

Product lines, and the top menu since 2026-10-09: **MOD** (modular house, the
main page, live), **POD** (capsule home, coming soon), **FAB** (prefab
building: workforce and institutional, coming soon), and **Contact**.
**Partnership** (micro manufacturers, farmhouse promoters, local
contractors), **Investors** and **Technology** are in a band at the foot of
every page; only Partnership has a page. The site is "powered by
Hemisphere One" (logo supplied 2026-10-09, shown in that band; since
2026-10-10 the words also stand under the wordmark everywhere and, with
the logo, on the opening card). Where the company is the subject of a
sentence it is "VEE" or "VEE.BUILD", not "we" (client, 2026-10-10).

The client's line for each kind (2026-10-09):

## Audience

Vacation-home and farmhouse buyers; urban infill and multi-family investors;
commercial developers and student-housing operators; employers needing
workforce accommodation; institutions; prospective manufacturing and
distribution partners.

## What the visitor must leave with

1. I understand what VEE.BUILD does.
2. I understand how the modular system works.
3. I understand why it is different.
4. I can see what can be built with it.
5. I trust this is a real construction company.
6. I want to enquire.

The client's one line for the last three chapters (2026-10-10):
**Manufacture → Finish → Lift → Transport → Install → Assemble →
Illuminate.** The factory makes and finishes one shell and cranes it onto

the lights come on last.

## Terminology from the brief (use exactly)

- Shell A / Shell B; Hook & Cleat (corner system).
- Foundation: Plug footing, L plinth, Jack pier (height set on the jack thread).
- Insulation: structural shell, perimeter metal frame, thicker panels on the
  exterior face, thinner panels as interior partitions, openings for main
  door, windows and internal access.
- Add-ons: deck platform, deck post, spandrel, handrail, steps, window/door
  shades, sloped roof option, roof solar arrays.
- Stacked configurations: The Villa, The Apartment, and the five-floor
  block, which is the **Mid-Rise Apartment** since 2026-10-09 (the brief's
  "High-Rise Block"; "high-rise" is no longer used). "How it works" shows
  the villa and the mid-rise; the configurator has all three as presets.
- The module's three parts (client, 2026-10-09): structural frame (steel),
  insulated panels (3D EPS), micro concrete. "Lift & shift" modules;
  "building as a product"; "building-kit-as-parts".
- The three layers of a building: Foundation. Module. Add-ons.
- Factory stations, as the client set them on 2026-10-10: Structural
  frame – core shell (on adjustable piers), insulation panels, formwork
  (bright green boards), concreting cycle, de-shuttering, doors and
  windows, spray finish; then dispatch. (The brief's "factory gates" and
  "assembly bays" are no longer shown.)
- Installation: "Placed on the foundation" (not "lifted onto the piers").
- Add-ons are optional: the deck, the steps and the roof are tagged so.
- Capsule home (the brief's "capsule pods"): 10′, 16′ and 20′ long.
- Spelling: -ize ("standardized", "componentized"), as the client writes.
- Institutional: 36′ long prefab / precast wall panels, G+2.
- Workforce housing: G+1.

  door; where the front is built against, it moves to a free face.
